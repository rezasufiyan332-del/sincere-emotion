# Stop Chasing, Start Choosing

## A 7-Day Guide to Healing Your Attachment Wounds

**By Sophie — Relationship Coach & Attachment Specialist**

---

## Introduction: Why This Book Exists

I spent seven years chasing people who couldn't love me back.

Not because they were cruel. Not because they didn't care. But because they were emotionally unavailable — and I was addicted to the chase.

I called it love. I called it passion. I called it "fighting for the relationship."

But the truth was simpler and more painful: I was anxious, and I was choosing people who confirmed my deepest fear — that I wasn't enough.

If you're reading this, you probably understand. You've probably lain awake at 2 AM wondering why they didn't text back. You've probably replayed conversations in your head, looking for the moment you "ruined" things. You've probably wondered if there's something fundamentally wrong with you.

There isn't.

You're not broken. You're not too much. You're not "crazy."

You're anxiously attached. And once you understand what that means, everything changes.

This book is your first step.

## Day 1: The Mirror — Understanding Your Pattern

### The Question Nobody Asks

Most relationship advice starts with "pick better partners." But nobody asks why you keep picking the same type.

Here's what I've learned after coaching hundreds of people: we don't attract what we want. We attract what we're familiar with.

If you grew up with inconsistent love — a parent who was sometimes present, sometimes distant — your nervous system learned that love is unpredictable. And now, as an adult, you unconsciously seek out relationships that feel familiar. Even when familiar means painful.

**Today's Exercise: The Relationship Audit**

Write down your last three romantic interests. For each one, answer:

1. Were they consistently available, or was there a push-pull dynamic?
2. Did you feel secure, or were you constantly wondering where you stood?
3. Did they meet your needs, or were you always giving more than you received?

If you see a pattern — if you notice you're always the one chasing, always the one wondering, always the one anxious — that's not a coincidence. That's your attachment style showing up.

**Key Insight:** Your anxiety in relationships isn't a character flaw. It's a learned response. And learned responses can be unlearned.

## Day 2: The Science — What Is Anxious Attachment?

### Your Brain on Love (Literally)

Attachment theory was developed by John Bowlby in the 1950s. It explains how our earliest bonds with caregivers shape our adult relationships.

There are three main attachment styles:

**Secure Attachment (about 50% of people):**
- Comfortable with intimacy and independence
- Can communicate needs clearly
- Trusts that others will be there for them

**Anxious Attachment (about 20% of people):**
- Craves deep intimacy but fears abandonment
- Often feels "not enough"
- Hyper-sensitive to any sign of withdrawal
- Tends to overthink and over-give

**Avoidant Attachment (about 25% of people):**
- Values independence over connection
- Uncomfortable with emotional closeness
- Withdraws when things get "too serious"
- May seem caring but keeps distance

The remaining 5% have disorganized attachment, which is a combination of anxious and avoidant patterns.

### Why You Chase

When you're anxiously attached, your brain is wired to detect threats to the relationship. A delayed text isn't just a delayed text — it's evidence that they're losing interest. A busy day isn't just a busy day — it's proof that you're not important.

Your nervous system goes into survival mode. And in survival mode, you do what you've always done: you chase. You text again. You ask "are we okay?" You try harder. You give more.

But here's the cruel irony: the harder you chase, the more they pull away. And the more they pull away, the harder you chase.

**Key Insight:** Your anxiety isn't about the other person. It's about your nervous system. And you can rewire it.

## Day 3: The Triggers — What Sets You Off

### The 5 Most Common Anxious Triggers

Understanding your triggers is the first step to managing them. Here are the five most common triggers for anxiously attached people:

**1. The Slow Response**
When they don't text back quickly, your mind starts spinning. "Are they mad? Did I say something wrong? Are they with someone else?"

**2. The Vague Answer**
When they give short or unclear responses, you read between the lines. "They said 'I'm fine' but they're clearly not fine. What did I do?"

**3. The Emotional Distance**
When they seem withdrawn or distant, you feel it physically. Your chest tightens. Your stomach churns. You feel abandoned even when they're right there.

**4. The Independence**
When they do things without you — go out with friends, take a trip, have their own life — you feel threatened. "If they don't need me, they'll leave me."

**5. The Conflict**
When there's disagreement or tension, you panic. You'll say anything, do anything, to make it go away. Because conflict feels like the beginning of the end.

**Today's Exercise: Your Trigger List**

Identify which of these five triggers affect you most. Write down a recent situation where each trigger showed up. Notice the pattern between the trigger, your thoughts, and your behavior.

**Key Insight:** Triggers aren't random. They're predictable. And predictable things can be managed.

## Day 4: The Body — Regulating Your Nervous System

### Your Body Keeps the Score

Here's something nobody tells you about anxiety: it lives in your body, not just your mind.

When you're triggered, your body goes into fight-or-flight mode. Your heart races. Your breathing becomes shallow. Your muscles tense. Your digestive system shuts down.

This isn't weakness. This is biology. Your body is responding to a perceived threat — and in your nervous system, relationship abandonment IS a threat. It's the same response our ancestors had when they were separated from their tribe.

The good news? You can regulate your body. And when you regulate your body, your thoughts follow.

**The 4-7-8 Breathing Technique**

This is the fastest way to calm your nervous system:

1. Breathe in through your nose for 4 seconds
2. Hold your breath for 7 seconds
3. Breathe out through your mouth for 8 seconds
4. Repeat 3-4 times

Do this when you feel triggered. Not later. Not when you "have time." Right now.

**The Cold Water Reset**

When you're spiraling, splash cold water on your face. This activates the dive reflex, which slows your heart rate and activates your parasympathetic nervous system. It's like hitting the reset button on your body.

**Key Insight:** You can't think your way out of anxiety. You have to feel your way through it.

## Day 5: The Mind — Challenging Anxious Thoughts

### The Stories You Tell Yourself

Anxious attachment comes with a lot of mental narratives. These stories feel like truth, but they're actually interpretations. And interpretations can be changed.

**Common Anxious Narratives:**

"They're pulling away because I'm too much."
→ Evidence: They had a busy day at work. They texted you goodnight. They said they'd call tomorrow.

"If I don't respond immediately, they'll lose interest."
→ Evidence: They took 3 hours to respond last week, and nothing changed. Healthy relationships don't require instant responses.

"They don't care about me as much as I care about them."
→ Evidence: You've never actually asked them. You're assuming based on your fear, not their actions.

**The Thought Record Technique**

When you have an anxious thought, write it down in four columns:

1. **Situation:** What happened?
2. **Thought:** What did you tell yourself?
3. **Emotion:** What did you feel?
4. **Evidence:** What's the actual evidence for and against this thought?

Over time, you'll start to see that most of your anxious thoughts are not supported by evidence. They're supported by fear.

**Key Insight:** Your thoughts are not facts. They're just thoughts.

## Day 6: The Communication — Speaking Your Truth

### Why You Don't Say What You Mean

Anxious attachment makes communication hard. You want to express your needs, but you're afraid of:

- Being "too much"
- Pushing them away
- Starting a fight
- Being abandoned

So you don't say anything. You suppress. You accommodate. You pretend you're fine when you're not.

And then one day, you explode. Or you shut down. Or you leave — not because you want to, but because you can't hold it in anymore.

**The Framework: Feel, Need, Request**

When you need to communicate something difficult, use this structure:

**Feel:** "I feel [emotion] when [specific situation]."
**Need:** "I need [specific need] in our relationship."
**Request:** "Would you be willing to [specific, actionable request]?"

Example:
"I feel anxious when I don't hear from you for several hours. I need to know you're thinking of me. Would you be willing to send a quick text when you're busy so I know you haven't disappeared?"

This is not being needy. This is being clear. And clarity is kindness.

**Key Insight:** Your needs are not emergencies. They're information.

## Day 7: The Future — Choosing Yourself

### The Most Important Relationship

Here's what I wish someone had told me seven years ago: the most important relationship you'll ever have is the one with yourself.

When you're anxiously attached, you focus all your energy on the other person. You study their moods, predict their behavior, adjust yourself to fit their needs. And in the process, you forget who you are.

You forget what you want. You forget what you need. You forget that you matter.

**The Commitment to Self**

Today, make this commitment to yourself:

"I will treat myself with the same kindness I give to others. I will honor my needs. I will not abandon myself to keep someone else."

This doesn't mean being selfish. It means being self-aware. It means knowing that you deserve love that doesn't require you to shrink.
